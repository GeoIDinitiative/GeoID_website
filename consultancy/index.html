Specialist modelling, sized to the question

Most hazard questions don't need a year-long study. They need someone who can find the right data, choose a method that fits it, run it properly, and say plainly how far the answer can be trusted. That is what a focused assessment is.

The work draws on the tools that run GeoHUB — its catalogues, terrain, geology and six hazard models — and, where the question calls for it, finite-element modelling with the GALES solver, the code behind the Etna magma-dynamics research.

  • Led by Owen McCluskey, computational geoscientist (PhD, University of Pisa / INGV)
  • Finite-element modelling of magma dynamics, glaciology and volcanogenic floods
  • Regional landslide and flood screening validated against mapped inventories
  • Every deliverable reproducible: data, parameters and code versions recorded

What we assess

Six kinds of focused study. Most engagements are one of these over one area; some combine two where one hazard drives another, such as rainfall-triggered landslides feeding a debris flow.

01

Landslide & slope stability

Susceptibility mapping and infinite-slope Factor of Safety from terrain, geology and rainfall scenarios, checked against any landslide inventory for the area.

  • Factor-of-Safety maps
  • Rainfall triggers
  • Validation scores
02

Flood & inundation scenarios

Fluvial and surface-water inundation for chosen discharge or rainfall scenarios, with depth and extent mapped and compared with recorded flood outlines.

  • Depth & extent maps
  • Catchment analysis
  • Asset exposure
03

Volcanic hazard & ashfall

Ash dispersal and deposit thickness from eruption scenarios and real wind fields, lava-flow path screening, and the eruptive record of the volcano.

Seismic hazard context

Glacial & volcanogenic floods

How an engagement runs

Four stages, each ending in something you can read before the next begins. Nothing is charged until you have accepted a written proposal.

  1. Scoping call

    A free conversation about the site, the decision the assessment has to support, and the data that already exists. If a focused assessment is not the right tool, we say so.

  2. Written proposal

    The question, method, data, deliverables, timetable and a fixed fee — plus the known limitations of the approach, stated before any work starts.

  3. Modelling & checks

    Data compiled and reviewed, models built and run, results checked against observations wherever they exist. You see interim results before the report is written.

  4. Report & handover

    A written report with maps, an assumptions register and an uncertainty statement, the GIS layers, and a walk-through call, with time for questions afterwards.

Track record: scored, not asserted

The models behind the assessments are tested against events they were not built from, and the results are reported whether they flatter the model or not — including one where the answer was “this does not transfer”.

Area under the ROC curve on events after each model's training period: 0.5 is no skill, 1 a perfect ranking, and the bars run from 0.5 to 1. These are the screening models an assessment starts from, not the site-specific models it builds. Three cases follow.

GeoHUB's flood susceptibility model was trained on floods before 2005 and scored on the floods of 2005–2025 it had never seen — the way a client's question is really asked: what happens next?

A high headline score still misses floods. The miss rate goes in the report next to the hit rate, so you know how much weight a map can bear.

What you receive — and what an assessment is not

A focused assessment is a desk-based, model-led study. It is precise about what it covers and equally precise about what it does not, so that nobody relies on it for something it was never built to do.

Included

  • A written report: the question, method, data, results and conclusions
  • Hazard maps as figures and as GIS layers (GeoTIFF, GeoPackage or shapefile)
  • An assumptions register listing every simplification made
  • An uncertainty statement: what the result is sensitive to, and by how much
  • A data-source list with licences, versions and access dates
  • An optional private GeoHUB project to explore the results on the globe
  • A walk-through call and a clarification period after delivery

Not included unless agreed in writing

  • Intrusive site investigation, drilling, sampling or laboratory testing
  • Geotechnical or structural design, or sign-off of works
  • A flood risk assessment or other report for statutory or regulatory submission
  • Certification by a chartered engineer or chartered geologist
  • Real-time monitoring, early warning or emergency advice
  • Insurance, valuation, conveyancing or legal advice
  • Any guarantee that a hazard will or will not occur

Who it's for

Organisations that need a defensible first answer to a hazard question, quickly, from someone who will show their working.

Public bodies & NGOs

Screening an area you are responsible for, prioritising where detailed investigation should go, or preparing for a community conversation about risk.

Specialist hazard modelling as a subcontract, with your chartered staff retaining design responsibility and sign-off.

Questions clients ask

Anything else, ask in the enquiry form or by email — the scoping call is free.

How much does an assessment cost?

Each engagement has a fixed fee, agreed in writing before any work starts. The fee depends on the size of the area, the number of hazards, how much data already exists and what you need delivered. The scoping call is free and you are under no obligation afterwards.

Can the report be used for a planning application or building control?

Not on its own, unless the proposal expressly says it is being prepared for that purpose. Statutory submissions such as site-specific flood risk assessments usually need to follow a set format and be signed off by a suitably chartered professional. A GeoID assessment can be a valuable input to that work, and we are happy to work alongside the practice that takes responsibility for it.

Are you chartered?

No. The service is led by a computational geoscientist with doctoral research in numerical hazard modelling. Where a project needs chartered sign-off, we work as a specialist modelling subcontractor to a chartered engineer or geologist, who retains that responsibility. See clause 04 of the terms below.

Do you visit the site?

Assessments are desk-based by default, which keeps them quick and affordable. A site walkover can be added where it would change the answer, for example to check drainage or evidence of past movement. It is quoted separately. Intrusive investigation such as drilling or sampling is not offered.

What information should I have ready?

The location and extent of the site or area, what decision the assessment is meant to inform, your deadline, and anything you already hold: surveys, drawings, borehole logs, photographs, or records of past floods or slope movement. None of this is essential to start; the scoping call will identify what helps most.

Where do you work?

GeoID is based in Northern Ireland and works across the UK and Ireland and internationally. Most of the global datasets the models use cover the whole world, but data quality varies by country. Where local data are thin, the proposal says so before you commit.

How sure can I be of the results?

Every hazard model simplifies the ground, so every result carries uncertainty. Reports tell you how much: each one includes an assumptions register, an uncertainty statement and, where local records exist, a validation score. Read the results with those, and see clause 02 of the terms below.

What happens if you find a mistake after delivery, or I do?

We tell you promptly about any error we find that materially affects a conclusion. If you find one, report it within ninety days; if the error is ours and within scope, we correct and reissue the report at no cost, or refund the affected part. The details are in clause 06.

Will my site or my results appear in GeoHUB or the Journal?

No, not without your written consent. Client sites, data and results are confidential and are never placed in the public parts of GeoHUB. If you would like a private GeoHUB project to explore the results, it is set up for you alone.

01Nature of the service

Consultancy is provided by Owen McCluskey, trading as the GeoID Initiative, a sole trader established in Northern Ireland, United Kingdom (“GeoID”, “we”, “us”), together with any named collaborators set out in the proposal for a given project.

Unless the proposal expressly says otherwise, each assessment is a desk-based, screening-level study: it uses existing data and numerical or statistical models to estimate hazard. 02Models, assumptions & uncertainty

All modelling involves error. Results depend on choices of method, parameters and input data, each of which carries uncertainty, and on simplifications of physical processes that are more complex in reality. In particular, and without limitation:

  • Resolution. Terrain models, geological maps, soil and land-cover data have finite resolution and accuracy; features smaller than the data resolution — a culvert, a retaining wall, a local spring, a thin weak layer — may not be represented.
  • Parameters. Material properties (strength, permeability, roughness, density and similar) are usually taken from literature or regional values rather than measured at the site, and real values vary from place to place.
  • Scenarios and probabilities. Rainfall, discharge, eruption and ground-motion scenarios are chosen assumptions. A return period or exceedance probability describes long-run likelihood, not when an event will occur; an event larger than any scenario modelled can happen.
  • Change over time. Ground conditions, land use, drainage, vegetation, defences and climate change. Results describe conditions as represented by the data at the time of the assessment.
  • Validation limits. Where models are checked against recorded events, agreement with past events does not guarantee performance for future ones, and many areas have little or no record to check against.
  • Software. Models are run in research software, including GeoID's own tools and the GALES solver, which, like all software, may contain undetected defects.

Each report includes an assumptions register and an uncertainty statement setting out the principal assumptions and sensitivities for that study. Results should be read together with them and never quoted without them.
