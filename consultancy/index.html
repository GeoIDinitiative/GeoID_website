Specialist modelling, sized to the question

Most hazard questions don't need a year-long study. They need someone who can find the right data, choose a method that fits it, run it properly, and say plainly how far the answer can be trusted. That is what a focused assessment is.

The work draws on the same tools that run GeoHUB — its catalogues, its terrain and geology, and its six hazard models — and, where the question calls for it, on finite-element modelling with the GALES solver, the code behind the Etna magma-dynamics research.

  • Led by Owen McCluskey, computational geoscientist (PhD, University of Pisa / INGV)
  • Finite-element modelling of magma dynamics, glaciology and volcanogenic floods
  • Regional landslide and flood screening validated against mapped inventories
  • Every deliverable reproducible: data, parameters and code versions recorded

What we assess

Six kinds of focused study. Most engagements are one of these over one area; some combine two where one hazard drives another, such as rainfall-triggered landslides feeding a debris flow.

01

Landslide & slope stability

Susceptibility mapping and infinite-slope Factor of Safety from terrain, mapped geology and rainfall scenarios, checked against any landslide inventory that exists for the area.

  • Susceptibility and Factor-of-Safety maps
  • Rainfall-trigger scenarios
  • Validation statistics where an inventory exists
02

Flood & inundation scenarios

Fluvial and surface-water inundation for chosen discharge or rainfall scenarios over a catchment or a site, with depth and extent mapped and compared with any recorded flood outlines.

  • Depth and extent maps per scenario
  • Drainage and catchment analysis
  • Exposure of named assets or parcels
03

Volcanic hazard & ashfall

Ash dispersal and deposit thickness from eruption scenarios and real wind fields, lava-flow path screening on terrain, and the eruptive record of the volcano in question.

  • Ashfall thickness and footprint maps
  • Flow-path and inundation screening
  • Eruptive-history summary
04

Seismic hazard context

The instrumental and historical earthquake record around a site, mapped faults, and a published hazard model's ground-motion levels, drawn together for a desk study or planning context.

  • Catalogue and fault compilation
  • Ground-motion context from published models
  • 3D hypocentre visualisation
05

Glacial & volcanogenic floods

Outburst-flood and meltwater scenarios from ice-covered volcanoes and glacial lakes: source volumes, routing down the valley, and arrival times at points that matter.

  • Scenario routing and arrival times
  • Ice and terrain change from public data
  • Sensitivity to source volume
06

Bespoke numerical modelling

Where a screening model is not enough: a site-specific finite-element model built from the terrain up and run with GALES — meshing, boundary conditions, parameter study and a written method.

  • Mesh and model set-up from real terrain
  • Parameter and sensitivity runs
  • Method note suitable for peer review

How an engagement runs

Four stages, each ending in something you can read before the next begins. Nothing is charged until you have accepted a written proposal.

  1. Scoping call

    A free conversation about the site, the decision the assessment has to support, and the data that already exists. If a focused assessment is not the right tool, we will say so.

  2. Written proposal

    The question, the method, the data, the deliverables, the timetable and a fixed fee — plus the known limitations of the chosen approach, stated before any work starts.

  3. Data, modelling & checks

    Data compiled and reviewed, models built and run, results checked against observations wherever observations exist. You get an interim look at the results before the report is written.

  4. Report & handover

    A written report with maps, an assumptions register and an uncertainty statement, the GIS layers, and a walk-through call. A short period for clarification questions is included.

What you receive — and what an assessment is not

A focused assessment is a desk-based, model-led study. It is precise about what it covers and equally precise about what it does not, so that nobody relies on it for something it was never built to do.

Included

  • A written report: the question, method, data, results and conclusions
  • Hazard maps as figures and as GIS layers (GeoTIFF, GeoPackage or shapefile)
  • An assumptions register listing every simplification made
  • An uncertainty statement: what the result is sensitive to and by how much
  • A data-source list with licences, versions and access dates
  • An optional GeoHUB project so you can explore the results on the globe
  • A walk-through call and a clarification period after delivery

Not included unless agreed in writing

  • Intrusive site investigation, drilling, sampling or laboratory testing
  • Geotechnical or structural design, or sign-off of works
  • A flood risk assessment or other report for statutory or regulatory submission
  • Certification by a chartered engineer or chartered geologist
  • Real-time monitoring, early warning or emergency advice
  • Insurance, valuation, conveyancing or legal advice
  • Any guarantee that a hazard will or will not occur

Who it's for

Organisations that need a defensible first answer to a hazard question, quickly, from someone who will show their working.

Public bodies & NGOs

Screening an area you are responsible for, prioritising where detailed investigation should go, or preparing for a community conversation about risk.

Engineering & environmental consultancies

Researchers & universities

Modelling support for a project or proposal, numerical work with GALES, or a hazard layer built for a study region.

Developers & landowners

An early, desk-based view of the hazards around a site before committing to a full investigation by a chartered practice.

Every hazard model is a simplification of the ground, and every result carries error. A GeoID assessment is a reasoned, documented estimate made from the data and methods available at the time. It is not a measurement, a prediction that an event will or will not happen, or a guarantee of safety. 01Nature of the service

Consultancy is provided by Owen McCluskey, trading as the GeoID Initiative, a sole trader established in Northern Ireland, United Kingdom (“GeoID”, “we”, “us”), together with any named collaborators set out in the proposal for a given project.

Unless the proposal expressly says otherwise, each assessment is a desk-based, screening-level study: it uses existing data and numerical or statistical models to estimate hazard. It does not include site investigation, ground truthing, testing of materials, or inspection of structures. The scope of each engagement is limited to the question, area, hazards and scenarios stated in the accepted proposal; matters outside that scope have not been considered.

02Models, assumptions & uncertainty

All modelling involves error. Results depend on choices of method, parameters and input data, each of which carries uncertainty, and on simplifications of physical processes that are more complex in reality. In particular, and without limitation:

  • Resolution. Terrain models, geological maps, soil and land-cover data have finite resolution and accuracy; features smaller than the data resolution — a culvert, a retaining wall, a local spring, a thin weak layer — may not be represented.
  • Parameters. Material properties (strength, permeability, roughness, density and similar) are usually taken from literature or regional values rather than measured at the site, and real values vary from place to place.
  • Scenarios and probabilities. Rainfall, discharge, eruption and ground-motion scenarios are chosen assumptions. A return period or exceedance probability describes long-run likelihood, not when an event will occur; an event larger than any scenario modelled can happen.
  • Change over time. Ground conditions, land use, drainage, vegetation, defences and climate change. Results describe conditions as represented by the data at the time of the assessment.
  • Validation limits. Where models are checked against recorded events, agreement with past events does not guarantee performance for future ones, and many areas have little or no record to check against.
  • Software. Models are run in research software, including GeoID's own tools and the GALES solver, which, like all software, may contain undetected defects.

Each report includes an assumptions register and an uncertainty statement setting out the principal assumptions and sensitivities for that study. Results should be read together with them and never quoted without them.

04Not professional sign-off

Unless the proposal expressly states otherwise, an assessment is not:

  • a geotechnical, structural or civil-engineering design, or approval of any design or works;
  • a report prepared or certified by a chartered engineer or chartered geologist;
  • a site-specific flood risk assessment, ground investigation report or other document prepared for planning, building control or any other statutory or regulatory purpose;
  • a warning or forecast service, or advice for an emergency in progress; or
  • legal, financial, insurance, valuation or conveyancing advice.

Where a decision requires any of these, you should engage an appropriately qualified and insured professional, who may use our assessment as one input under their own professional responsibility. In an emergency, contact the emergency services or civil protection authority for your location.

05Use, reliance & validity

Each report is prepared for the client named in the proposal and for the purpose stated in it. It may be relied on only by that client, only for that purpose, and only in its entirety — extracts, individual maps or figures taken out of context may mislead.

No other person may rely on the report without our prior written consent. To the extent permitted by law, we accept no responsibility to any third party, and the Contracts (Rights of Third Parties) Act 1999 does not apply. If you share the report, you should make its limitations clear to the recipient.

Because conditions and data change, a report reflects the position at its date of issue. Unless the report states a different period, its conclusions should not be relied upon more than twelve months after issue, or after any significant change at the site, event, or new data, without being reviewed.

06Errors & corrections

We check our work, but mistakes in data handling, calculation, interpretation or reporting can occur despite reasonable care. If you believe a report contains an error, please tell us in writing as soon as possible and in any event within ninety days of delivery.

Where we agree that the report contains an error attributable to us and within the agreed scope, we will correct it and reissue the affected parts at no further cost, and tell you whether the correction changes any conclusion. If we cannot correct the error, we will refund the fee paid for the affected part of the work. Subject to clause 07, this is our principal remedy for errors in a delivered report.

If we discover an error ourselves after delivery that we believe materially affects a conclusion, we will tell you promptly.

10Confidentiality

We treat everything you share with us, and the results of your assessment, as confidential. We use it only to deliver the engagement and disclose it only to collaborators named in the proposal, who are bound by the same obligations, or where required by law. A separate non-disclosure agreement can be signed before the scoping call if you prefer.
