Data Sources

What GeoHUB Is Built From

Every layer on the globe comes from a published catalogue, a public feed or a mission dataset, and keeps its source, its licence and its DOI in the legend — so anything you derive from it inherits the same terms. This page lists them. The globe is where they are used; the Journal is where the models built on them are written up.

What the models use

The Inputs Behind Each Hazard Model

⛰️

Landslides & slope stability

Streamed DEM for slope and drainage, Macrostrat lithology with a Geological Strength Index and the geotechnical database for strength, and sixteen days of hourly NOAA GFS rainfall for the water balance.

🌧️

Flood, cyclone & sea level

Mapped channels and the DEM for inundation by discharge; every IBTrACS best-track since 1842 for cyclone return periods; the real coastline through the streamed heights for sea level. RainViewer radar and GFS forecast rainfall overlay the same ground live.

🌋

Seismic & volcanic

USGS ComCat, ISC-GEM and GEM GHEC merged into one record of 312,500 earthquakes for shaking by magnitude; the Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program Holocene catalogue, with its eruption sizes, for ashfall and hazard buffers.

Catalogues & feeds

On the Globe Today

🛰️

Earthquakes & cyclones

USGS ComCat; ISC-GEM and GEM GHEC (CC BY-SA 3.0), M4.5+ from 1008. IBTrACS v04r01, NOAA NCEI (Knapp et al. 2010), 13,513 tracks from 1842.

📡

Geology & volcanoes

Macrostrat world geology as vector tiles (CC BY 4.0), with rock properties and GSI derived from lithology; Smithsonian GVP volcanoes with graduated hazard buffers; active faults and plate boundaries.

☔

Live feeds

NASA EONET and USGS events by category; seismograms from the nearest FDSN station on click; CelesTrak orbital elements propagated every 1.5 seconds; RainViewer radar; NOAA GFS forecast rainfall.

🌊

Terrain, imagery & base data

Streamed elevation tiles and GEBCO bathymetry; Sentinel-2 cloudless 2025 by EOX (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0); Natural Earth coastlines, rivers and boundaries; Google Earth Engine's catalogue for members.

Why it matters

One Globe, Every Source Named

Readable by non-specialists

A catalogue that took an afternoon of downloads and reprojection arrives symbolised on a globe, with a popup per feature.

Useful for researchers

Draw an area and sample every layer into one table, export it, or extract the DEM and carry it into the Meshing Studio and the Research Hub inside GeoHUB.

Checkable

The models built on these inputs state their method beside the map and are written up in the Journal, so a result can be challenged against its sources.
