GeoID Hazard App

The Hazard Interface For Earth-System Monitoring

This is the hazard-facing application layer within GeoID. It organizes the repository's building blocks for geohazard awareness: weather model pipelines, radar and satellite precipitation sources, bathymetry, terrain products, shapefiles, and machine-learning artifacts that support floods, landslides, coastal impacts, and broader environmental change.

Open Initiative Website For Researchers

From data to warning context

GeoID’s hazard-facing side is about making datasets legible, navigable, and connected to real decisions rather than leaving them buried in raw directories.

Hazard Focus

Primary Awareness Domains

⛰️

Landslides & Slope Failure

Hydrogeology and landslide coursework, terrain data, and shapefile resources support slope stability reasoning, rainfall-trigger thresholds, and terrain-risk interpretation.

🌧️

Flooding & Rainfall

NOAA GFS, RainViewer, IMERG, radar pulls, and weather ML artifacts provide a foundation for precipitation-driven hazard awareness and event monitoring.

🌋

Volcanic Systems

Crash courses and planetary viewers create a route from conceptual volcanology to comparative planetary interpretation and terrestrial hazard framing.

Data Sources

Major Inputs Already In The Repository

🛰️

NOAA GFS

Forecast model inputs and weather-processing utilities inside `input/weather data/NOAA GFS`.

📡

RainViewer & Radar

Radar pull and RainViewer directories for precipitation event tracking and near-real-time environmental context.

☔

IMERG

Satellite precipitation inputs paired with processing logs and downstream ML artifacts.

🌊

GEBCO & Coastal Data

Bathymetry and coastal shapefile datasets for seabed, coastal, and terrain-informed situational analysis.

01
`input/weather data/` NOAA GFS, RainViewer, IMERG, radar pull pipelines, artifacts, and machine-learning stages for precipitation and forecast processing.
02
`input/shapefiles/` Coastal and survey shapefiles that can support mapping, exposure context, and terrain-aware project workflows.
03
`input/data/gebco` and `input/data/stl` Bathymetry and geometry-oriented resources that support geospatial interpretation and research preprocessing.
Why It Matters

Why The Hazard App Sits Apart From The Initiative

Readable by non-specialists

The platform can translate raw hazard and environmental inputs into navigable, understandable entry points for wider audiences.

Useful for researchers

The same data can feed directly into preprocessing, model building, interpretation, and comparison workflows through the Research Hub.

Connected to learning

Crash courses and Explorer viewers make GeoID a teaching environment as well as a monitoring interface, which raises hazard literacy instead of just presenting outputs.
