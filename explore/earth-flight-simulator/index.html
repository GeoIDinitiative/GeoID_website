Free flight simulator

Fly the Real Earth in Your Browser

Earth Flight puts a light aircraft or a helicopter anywhere on the planet and flies it over the real ground: satellite imagery on true terrain, OpenStreetMap buildings and trees, and towns and landmarks named as they come into view. No download, no account — open it and fly.

What you can do

Fly the Real Earth in Your Browser

Real ground, everywhere

Esri World Imagery down to street level, laid on streamed elevation so mountains, valleys and coastlines are where they really are.

A real night

After dark the imagery dims and street lamps appear along real OpenStreetMap roads — lit where NASA's Black Marble satellite data shows a town is lit.

Places as you fly

Cities and towns from high up; villages, landmarks, museums and historic sites as you come down, from OpenStreetMap and Wikipedia. Click one for its card.

Sky and weather

Day, dusk and night follow the real sun over where you are, with high cloud from the day's weather.

What you can fly

Choose a light aircraft or a helicopter, pick a launch height from 300 metres to orbit, and take off from wherever you are looking — or open one of the launch points below. The aircraft flies with the planet turning under it, the HUD reads altitude, speed, heading, temperature and pressure for where you are, and names the country and nearest town.

Fly with the keyboard or a game controller. The helicopter is held to its real service ceiling; the aircraft can climb as far as you like.

The GeoHUB comes with you

Earth Flight is part of the GeoHUB globe, so its layers fly too. Switch on world geology, a river network or an Earth Engine image and it is painted onto the terrain under the wings, hidden by the hills like the ground itself, and every feature can still be clicked for its card.

Questions

Good to Know

Is it free?

Yes. Earth Flight runs in the browser, with nothing to install and no account.

Can I fly over my own town?

Yes — anywhere on Earth. Look at the place on the globe and launch, or add &lat= and &lon= to the Earth Flight link.

What does it need to run?

A modern browser with WebGL on a desktop or laptop. The imagery and terrain stream as you fly, so a steady connection helps.

Data: Imagery: Esri World Imagery. Elevation: streamed Terrarium tiles. Buildings, roads and place names: © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL) via OpenFreeMap. Night lights: NASA Black Marble (VIIRS). Landmarks: Wikipedia (CC BY-SA). Every layer carries its source and licence in the GeoHUB legend; see Data Sources.
