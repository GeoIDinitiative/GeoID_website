From the catalogue to the model

Members can run the volcanic ashfall model — where at least a millimetre of ash falls, by eruption size, from every volcano in the Smithsonian Holocene record. For one volcano in depth, the Mount Etna explorer has its plumbing, seismicity and deformation in 3D.

Questions

Good to Know

Where is the volcano data from?

The Smithsonian Institution's Global Volcanism Program; live eruptions from NASA EONET.

Is it free?

The map, the hazard rings and the live eruptions are free; the ashfall model is part of membership.

Data: Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program; NASA EONET. Every layer carries its source and licence in the GeoHUB legend; see Data Sources.

Related Tools

Live natural events

Wildfires, storms, floods, volcanic eruptions and earthquakes as they are reported, from NASA EONET and USGS, on a 3D globe — each linked to its source record.

World geology map

An interactive world geology map on a 3D globe: click any unit for its age, lithology and rock properties, with faults, plate boundaries and rivers — free in your browser.

Geohazard models

Six hazard models in the browser — landslides, floods, tropical cyclones, seismic shaking, volcanic ashfall and sea level — computed over the area you choose, with methods stated.
