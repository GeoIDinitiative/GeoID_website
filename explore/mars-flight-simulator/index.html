Fly Over Mars

Pick a launch site anywhere on Mars and fly it: the planet's real relief from NASA's Mars Orbiter Laser Altimeter, with the Context Camera mosaic streamed tile by tile beneath you, from a low pass over the canyons to orbit.

What you can do

Fly Over Mars

Real Martian terrain

MOLA relief — the altimeter map every Mars landing site is chosen on — under every pass.

Imagery that sharpens as you descend

The CTX mosaic streams beneath the aircraft, so the closer you fly the more of the ground you see.

8 km to 300 km

Skim Valles Marineris or climb to orbit and watch the planet curve away.

Named features

The places on the skyline are flagged with their names and their range, the most significant first.

A map as well as a flight

The flight simulator lives inside the Mars viewer, so the same window has the planet's mapped geology, elevation profiles, named-feature search, a core cutaway and a raster calculator that floods the northern lowlands to test where an ancient ocean might have stood.

Questions

Good to Know

Is the terrain real?

Yes: it is NASA's MOLA elevation, with Context Camera images streamed over it.

Is it free?

Yes, in the browser, with no account.

Data: Relief: NASA MGS MOLA. Imagery: MRO Context Camera (CTX) mosaic. Feature names: IAU Gazetteer of Planetary Nomenclature. Every layer carries its source and licence in the GeoHUB legend; see Data Sources.
