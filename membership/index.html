Membership

GeoID Explorer is open to everyone, and stays that way. Membership is for the part that is work rather than looking.

Free, for everybody, always

Every globe — Earth and nine other worlds — with its imagery, its terrain and its measurements. The geological, soil and surface-lithology maps. The glacier inventory, the volcano catalogue, the earthquake record, the cyclone tracks. The live feeds: earthquakes as they are reported, eruptions, wildfires, floods, storms. The satellites overhead. Drawing, measuring, clipping and the forty-eight analysis tools.

No account, no sign-in, no payment, and nothing withheld from a reader who never makes one.

What membership adds

  • The modelled risk maps The hazard models GeoID computes itself, rather than redistributes: tropical cyclone and seismic risk, volcanic ashfall by eruption size, the forecast landslide and flood models, river flood inundation by scenario and by discharge, river corridor zones, and sea level. Each shows its own working — every equation, every assumption and every source is on the layer.
  • Saving and exporting Keep a project as a real folder on your own disk — the same twenty directories the desktop Research app uses, field for field, so a study opens in either — reopen it later, and export what you make from it.

Your work is yours, and we never hold it

There is no server of ours holding anybody's study. A GeoID project is a folder you chose on your own machine, or this browser's own storage, or your own computer through the sidecar. Nothing in the app uploads one, and membership does not change that: it is an entitlement, not a place we put your work.

What we keep is your verified email address and the date your membership runs to. Sign-in is Google's or GitHub's, so there is no password of ours to hold, to leak, or for you to reset.

The consequence worth knowing: if you lose the folder, we cannot get it back for you. That is the same trade a word processor makes, and it is the price of not being asked to hand your research to anybody.

Why these maps and not the others

The dividing line is not how useful a layer is — it is whose work it is. A map somebody else published under an open licence is theirs to give away, and we pass it on: Pelletier's soil thickness, WorldPop's population, the MERRA-2 climate normals, every survey and every catalogue. The maps behind membership are the ones this app models itself, from the ground up, and keeps honest.

Become a member

Payment is handled by Stripe. We never see your card — what reaches us is the email address you paid with, which is what the membership is attached to. Use the same address you sign in with, or tell us which one to attach it to.

Managing your membership

Your receipt from Stripe carries a link to their billing portal, where you can change your card, download invoices, or cancel. Cancelling stops the renewal and leaves the year you have paid for running to its end.
