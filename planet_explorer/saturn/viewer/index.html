Using The Saturn Explorer

Navigating The Globe

Orbit

Left-click and drag to rotate the globe freely in any direction.

Zoom

Scroll wheel or pinch to zoom in and out, revealing cloud-top banding and atmospheric detail.

Pan

Right-click and drag (or two-finger drag on trackpad) to pan the camera target.

Reset

Use the reset button beside the Saturn title in the top-left panel header to return to the default framing and clear any active tour or moon viewer.

Control Panel Layout

The left panel runs from navigation to interpretation. Tour Mode steps through curated stop sequences by theme. Locations controls surface labels by category. Basemap & Relief sets the globe texture and cloud displacement. Core View shows Saturn's layered interior structure. Moon Viewer flies to Saturn's major moons. The bottom-right HUD shows live cursor latitude, longitude, and map scale.

Tour Mode

Enable Tour Mode from the panel and choose a theme — storm systems, spacecraft encounters, polar regions, ring features, or atmospheric bands. The viewer flies automatically to each stop in sequence. Use the Prev and Next buttons or the target dropdown to navigate. Your basemap and overlay settings are preserved across stops.

Moon Viewer

Select one of Saturn's major moons — Titan, Enceladus, Mimas, Tethys, Dione, Rhea, or Iapetus — from the Moon Viewer section to fly out to the moon in close orbit. The panel focuses to show moon-specific labels including craters, chasms, and geological features. Click any moon feature label for its description and orbital data.

Datasets Available

Global basemaps

Saturn body colour map derived from Cassini and Hubble imagery, with band contrast and storm contrast enhancement layers.

Atmosphere overlays

Enhanced atmospheric context layer derived from the official NASA Saturn image, emphasising banding and cloud structure.

Ring system

Saturn's ring plane rendered as a textured disc with named ring regions from D ring to E ring, including the Cassini Division and Encke Gap.

Major moons

Seven major moons with textured globes: Titan, Enceladus, Mimas, Tethys, Dione, Rhea, and Iapetus, each with named surface features.

Storm and atmospheric features

Named atmospheric zones including the North Polar Hexagon, Great White Spots, Ribbon Wave, south polar vortex, and major belt and zone bands.

Spacecraft encounters

Reference points for Pioneer 11, Voyager 1, Voyager 2, and Cassini encounters, including the Cassini Grand Finale orbital entry region.

Measurements & Tools

Open the Tools panel from the right-hand tab. Point distance measures the great-circle arc between two cloud-top clicks. Area measurement computes the enclosed polygon area. The profile tool draws a cross-section along a dragged path. All measurement results can be exported as CSV or PNG.

About Saturn

Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second largest in the Solar System, with an equatorial radius of ~60,268 km. Its iconic ring system spans up to ~480,000 km from the planet's centre and consists primarily of water ice particles. Saturn's atmosphere is dominated by hydrogen and helium with ammonia ice clouds visible at the ~1 bar pressure level. The planet has at least 146 known moons — seven of which are large enough to be nearly spherical.
