Why myGeoID

A Broken Early Warning Landscape

Exposure to geohazards is rising; access to risk intelligence is not. People still depend on black‑box insurance assessments, delayed alerts, and fragmented systems.

myGeoID flips that model: a forecasted Factor of Safety that is readable, shareable, and actionable — with transparent methods that let people challenge bad quotes and biased assumptions.

Proposed Methodology

Turning Earth Science into Early Warnings

Earth observation data, geomechanical modelling, and machine learning in one digital twin — a continuously updated Factor of Safety (FoS) across multiple hazards. Methods will be published openly.

Forecast-Driven Risk Computation (Proposed)

Built on GFS NOAA global forecast data, myGeoID will compute a modelled Factor of Safety that anticipates instability before it is visible — earlier alerts, longer lead times.

Advanced Mathematical Models

Finite element modelling (FEM) — best practice for simulating stress, deformation, and failure — augmented by machine learning to flag emerging instability early.

Bridging Science and Society

A direct bridge from raw monitoring data to actionable public information. All you need is the app and an internet connection.

Our Vision

Democratizing Geohazard Science

Critical hazard data — accessible, portable, and globally distributed.

The myGeoID App Experience

A clean map view, locality search, and a single Factor of Safety signal turn complex models into a clear, shareable risk readout.

Market-style time‑series charts show how forecast inputs shift risk ahead of impact — the same evidence base experts use, open to everyone.

Roadmap

Proposed Development Path

From fundamental research to validated operational pilots — pending funding and collaboration.

Proposed Pilot

Ireland & Northern Ireland — Proposed Pilot

First deployment would unify landslide, flood, and slope stability risk across Northern Ireland and coastal Ireland — validating real-world performance before global scaling.

Planned Module

Flood Risk — Planned Module

Hydro-meteorological inputs feed the digital twin to surface FoS trends signalling emerging flood risk.

Landslide

Landslides & Slope Instability

Terrain change and rainfall loading feed FoS computation of slope stability and landslide susceptibility.
