What is the GeoID Explorer?

GeoID Explorer is an interactive planetary science platform built to make the solar system tangible, navigable, and scientifically meaningful for anyone — regardless of background or prior knowledge. It gives you eight fully immersive planetary viewers, one for each world from Mercury to Neptune, each built as a dedicated investigation environment layered with real scientific data: topographic models, geological maps, atmospheric imagery, mineral distribution layers, and mission-linked surface features. Rather than reducing planets to static images or memorised facts, GeoID Explorer lets you move through them directly — zooming into canyon systems on Mars, tracing volcanic provinces on Venus, tracking storm bands across Jupiter — building genuine intuition about scale, composition, and the forces that shape worlds. GeoID was founded on the principle that planetary science and space exploration should be as open and engaging as any other field, and the Explorer is the practical expression of that principle: an interface designed to close the distance between curious minds and the worlds orbiting our Sun.

Explore our Solar System

🚀

At its core, GeoID Explorer gives you eight fully interactive planetary viewers — one for every world from Mercury to Neptune. Each viewer is an investigation environment built around real scientific datasets: topographic models, geological maps, atmospheric imagery, mineral distribution layers, and mission-linked surface features.

Rather than presenting planets as static images or memorised facts, the Explorer lets you navigate their surfaces and structures directly, building intuition about scale, composition, and the processes that shape worlds. Every layer has been designed with accessibility in mind — whether you are a student encountering Mars for the first time or a researcher cross-referencing geological units, the platform scales to meet you. Rocky-planet viewers offer stacked comparative datasets designed for guided discovery; gas-giant viewers frame exploration around atmospheric dynamics, storm systems, and moon environments. Each world introduces concepts through visible evidence rather than abstracted diagrams.

The platform will continue to grow. New scientific layers, expanded moon environments, and deeper integration with current mission data are all in active development — every addition built toward the same goal of making space science genuinely accessible and interactive for everyone.
