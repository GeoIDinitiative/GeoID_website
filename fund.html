Research & Funding Prospectus

The platforms are live. Forecasting is one computational step away.

Two GeoID platforms are live and in use today. The next goal — physics-based hydrological models driven by live rainfall forecasts, producing a public flood and landslide risk index — needs servers and HPC access.

STATUS: The Explorer platforms are live. The forecasting system is the funding case, not a live service — the myGeoID prototype demos the intended interface.

What exists today

Two live platforms

Working, publicly accessible tools built from real scientific datasets — proof GeoID can build and sustain accessible, interactive geoscience.

Live today

Planet Explorer

Ten viewers spanning every planet — real topography, scientific overlays, guided tours — with a working membership and billing stack.

Live today

Earth Explorer — Etna

A 3D geological viewer from published INGV data: seismicity catalogue (2011–2023), fault planes, geological map, magma plumbing, lava flows, interactive cross-section. A research GIS in a browser.

Ingested

Hazard data pipelines

NOAA GFS forecasts, IMERG precipitation, RainViewer radar, GEBCO bathymetry, and geological shapefiles already ingested — not yet served continuously.

The immediate goal

How does land respond to forecasted rainfall?

NOAA GFS delivers global rainfall forecasts at 0.25° resolution every six hours. The question: how will a hillslope or catchment respond?

A finite-element hydrological model — the GALES multi-physics solver lineage applied to slope stability and flood routing — outputs a Factor of Safety: stable, marginal, or failing, computed before the rainfall arrives.

The science is established. A public, continuously running, free implementation is not. GeoID is building it first.

Constraint 1 — Servers

Continuous ingestion and a public interface need always-on infrastructure. The pipeline is tested; persistent hosting does not yet exist.

Constraint 2 — Computational Capacity

Continuous FEM runs at useful resolution require HPC allocation or dedicated cluster time — the principal cost this prospectus addresses.

The plan

Phases of development

Phase 0 — Platform and capability proof

Complete · Live

Ship accessible geoscience tools at production quality.

  • Planet Explorer: ten planetary viewers with overlays, tours, and membership billing
  • Earth Explorer — Etna: 3D geological viewer from published INGV datasets
  • Hazard data ingestion: NOAA GFS, IMERG, radar, GEBCO, shapefiles

Infrastructure: static hosting — already covered.

Phase 1 — Live GFS ingestion and first hydrological model

Seeking funding

Run the first continuous GFS → FEM → Factor of Safety pipeline for a pilot domain.

  • Always-on server for GFS ingestion, storage, and serving
  • FEM slope-stability and flood-routing model driven by live GFS forecasts
  • Public Factor of Safety output for the pilot domain
  • Validation against historical rainfall events and published landslide catalogues

Infrastructure: managed server plus HPC or GPU allocation for FEM runs — the core funding ask.

Phase 2 — Scale, public interface, and regional expansion

Seeking funding

Deliver the public myGeoID experience and expand beyond the pilot domain.

  • Personalised risk dashboards with saved locations and alerts (myGeoID)
  • Additional pilot regions by partner and funder priority
  • Open data-access layer and reproducible outputs for researchers
  • Further hazard types as the FEM lineage extends

Infrastructure: scaled hosting and storage as coverage grows.

How to get involved

Engagement models

A non-exclusive, open-science initiative. HPC access and funded research partnerships help most — all routes below are open.

High Performance Computing (HPC) & cloud in-kind

HPC allocations or cloud credits covering the Phase 1 FEM workloads.

Research grant co-application

Named partner or host institution on hazard, climate-resilience, or open-science calls.

University & data partnership

HPC time, validation data, or supervision — in return, a live research testbed and open teaching resources.

Institutional sponsorship

Civil-protection agencies, NGOs, or foundations seeking public-benefit early warning in underserved regions.

The Explorer membership (£19.99/year) shows public demand — it is not, and is not presented as, the financing mechanism for the forecasting platform.
