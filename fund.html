Research & Funding Prospectus

Operational geohazard intelligence is buildable — and underfunded.

GeoID proposes a free, public, physics-based early-warning platform for floods, landslides, and volcanic hazards. The science and the engineering capability are proven. What remains is the compute and infrastructure to run it at scale — and that is what this prospectus sets out to fund.

Discuss a partnership → See the capability proof (Etna)

Honest status. The public Hazard Intelligence platform described below is at concept and prototype stage — it is the subject of this funding case, not a live service. Everything cited as proof of capability (Planet Explorer and the Earth Explorer: Etna viewer) is live and usable today. We draw that line deliberately: funders should be able to verify exactly what exists versus what investment will create.

The Gap

Early warning is reactive, fragmented, and unevenly distributed

Geohazard monitoring today is split across agencies, formats, and access tiers. Forecast-grade tooling exists, but it is concentrated in well-resourced regions and rarely reaches the communities most exposed to floods, slope failure, and volcanic unrest. The scientific methods to compute risk ahead of an event — coupling live environmental inputs to physics-based models — are mature in research settings but seldom operationalised as a continuous, public-facing service.

GeoID's thesis is that the missing piece is not the science. It is the infrastructure and integration to run that science continuously, openly, and at scale. That is a fundable problem with a definable cost.

Proven Capability

What already exists — evidence the team can deliver

The assets below are not the funded product. They are deliberately scoped, live deliverables that demonstrate GeoID can build rigorous, performant, scientifically credible geoscience software — and has already ingested real hazard data. They de-risk the proposal.

Live today

Earth Explorer — Etna

A 3D geological viewer with the INGV earthquake catalogue, magma plumbing, fault planes, geology and hillshade overlays, historical lava flows, and an interactive cross-section. The clearest single demonstration of domain capability.

Live today

Planet Explorer

Ten interactive planetary and lunar viewers with layered scientific overlays, guided tours, and feature catalogues — proof of sustained, polished delivery and a working membership/auth/billing stack around it.

Ingested

Hazard data pipelines

Working ingestion of NOAA GFS, IMERG, RainViewer/radar, GEBCO bathymetry, and shapefile resources. The raw material for an operational system is already flowing — it is not yet served continuously.

What Funding Builds

A phased roadmap from proven capability to public service

Each phase has discrete, verifiable deliverables and a stated infrastructure dependency. Funding is sought from Phase 1 onward; Phase 0 is complete and self-funded.

Phase 0 — Capability proof

Complete · Live

Demonstrate scientific and engineering competence; validate that a single, coherent platform can render real hazard and planetary data well.

  • Etna 3D geological viewer with live INGV seismicity
  • Ten-body Planet Explorer suite with layered overlays and tours
  • Initial hazard-data ingestion across five external sources
  • Working accounts, authentication, and subscription billing

Infrastructure: static hosting and client-side rendering — minimal cost, already covered.

Phase 1 — Operational data backbone

Seeking funding

Move from "data ingested once" to "data served continuously" for a first monitored pilot region.

  • Automated, scheduled ingestion and storage of live environmental feeds
  • A first continuously monitored pilot domain with a public hazard dashboard
  • Baseline threshold-based alerting (rainfall, seismicity, slope indicators)
  • Provenance, versioning, and an open data-access layer

Infrastructure: always-on managed servers, scheduled compute, and durable storage for time-series and raster archives.

Phase 2 — Physics-based modelling at scale

Seeking funding

Couple the GALES multi-physics finite-element lineage to live inputs so risk is computed ahead of events, not just reported after.

  • Operationalised flood and slope-stability models driven by live forecasts
  • Volcanic deformation and unrest modelling extending the Etna proof
  • Reproducible model runs with versioned outputs for researchers
  • Validation against historical events and published catalogues

Infrastructure: this is the compute-intensive step — HPC allocation or a GPU cluster for FEM workloads. The principal cost driver behind this prospectus.

Phase 3 — Public early-warning service & Research Hub

Seeking funding

Deliver the public-facing myGeoID experience and open the platform to the research community.

  • Personalised dashboards, saved locations, and custom alert profiles (myGeoID)
  • Regional expansion beyond the pilot domain
  • Researcher workflow tooling: project setup, preprocessing, solver configuration, outputs
  • Shareable hazard reports and an open educational resource layer

Infrastructure: scaled hosting, notification delivery, and sustained storage growth as coverage and users expand.

Why Infrastructure Is The Ask

The cost is not the code — it is running the science continuously

🧮

Compute for physics

Multi-physics FEM simulation is inherently heavy. Computing risk ahead of events needs HPC or GPU resources that exceed what static hosting or a single workstation can provide.

🛰️

Always-on ingestion

Live forecast, radar, and satellite feeds must be pulled, processed, and stored on a schedule, indefinitely. That requires managed servers running continuously, not on demand.

📈

Room to grow

Every new monitored region multiplies storage and compute. Sustainable public coverage means provisioning for growth, not just a one-off demo.

On The Membership

The Explorer membership validates demand — it does not fund the platform

GeoID offers a low-cost annual membership for the educational Planet Explorer viewers. We are explicit with funders about its role: at realistic early-stage scale, subscription revenue is not, and is not presented as, the financing mechanism for the hazard platform. Its purpose is to demonstrate genuine public appetite for accessible geoscience tools — engagement and retention that strengthen the case for institutional investment. The membership is the evidence of demand; the funding this prospectus seeks is the means of supply.
