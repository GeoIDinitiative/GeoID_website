Roadmap & Partnerships

The models are live. Running them continuously is the next step.

GeoHUB is live and free, and its six hazard models are open to members today, computed on demand over the area a reader chooses. The next goal — the same landslide and flood models running every forecast cycle on hosted compute, with saved locations and alerts — needs servers and HPC time. This page is the case for that step, and the ways to take part.

STATUS: GeoHUB, Earth Explorer and the membership models are live. Continuous hosted forecasting is the roadmap item this page is about, not a live service; the models remain screening-level research tools and are not a certified or operational warning system.

What exists today

Live, in use, and paying its way

Working, publicly accessible tools built from the source catalogues, with a membership that already funds their hosting.

Live today

GeoHUB

A hazard GIS on a 3D globe: 312,500 earthquakes back to 1008, 13,513 cyclone tracks, Macrostrat geology, Smithsonian volcanoes, live EONET and USGS events, drawn study areas sampled to a table, the Meshing Studio and the Research Hub — and ten worlds in the same window.

Live today

Earth Explorer — Etna

A 3D geological viewer from published INGV data: seismicity catalogue (2011–2023), fault planes, geological map, magma plumbing, lava flows, interactive cross-section. A research GIS in a browser.

Live today

The hazard models — membership

Six modelled maps on the same globe: landslide and slope stability from sixteen days of GFS rainfall, flood by discharge, cyclone, seismic, volcanic ashfall and sea level; with rock properties, the Model Builder and Earth Engine. £49.99 a year, with Stripe billing and sign-in live.

Published

The methods

Three technical notes in the Journal: the forecast landslide and flood model from GFS rainfall, probabilistic ashfall by eruption size, and the merged global earthquake record with its per-magnitude shaking maps.

The immediate goal

From on demand to continuous

NOAA GFS delivers global rainfall forecasts at 0.25° resolution every six hours. Today a member picks an area and GeoHUB runs the landslide and flood models over it in the browser, for the next sixteen days of that forecast.

The next step is to run the same models — and the finite-element slope and flood-routing models in the GALES lineage behind them — for every forecast cycle, over whole regions, on a server: a Factor of Safety that is already computed when somebody opens the map, with saved locations and alerts on top.

The science is established and the models exist. A public, continuously running implementation is what remains.

Constraint 1 — Servers

Continuous ingestion and a public output need always-on infrastructure. The pipeline runs on demand today; persistent hosting for the continuous version does not yet exist.

Constraint 2 — Computational Capacity

Continuous FEM runs at useful resolution require HPC allocation or dedicated cluster time — the principal cost this prospectus addresses.

The plan

Phases of development

Phase 0 — The workspace and the models

Complete · Live

Ship the globe, the record and the models at production quality, and sell the models.

  • GeoHUB: the merged hazard record, world geology, live feeds, drawing and sampling, the Meshing Studio, the Research Hub, ten worlds
  • Earth Explorer — Etna and ASCENT: 3D site models from published datasets
  • Six hazard models on demand, with rock properties, the Model Builder and Earth Engine, behind a live membership
  • The methods published as technical notes in the Journal

Infrastructure: static hosting — already covered.

Phase 1 — Live GFS ingestion and first hydrological model

Seeking funding

Run the landslide and flood models continuously, every GFS cycle, for a pilot region.

  • Always-on server for GFS ingestion, storage, and serving
  • The existing models, and the FEM slope-stability and flood-routing models behind them, driven by each live GFS cycle
  • A precomputed Factor of Safety for the pilot region, open when the map opens
  • Validation against historical rainfall events and published landslide catalogues

Infrastructure: managed server plus HPC or GPU allocation for FEM runs — the core funding ask.

Phase 2 — Scale, public interface, and regional expansion

Seeking funding

Saved locations and alerts inside GeoHUB, and more regions.

  • Saved locations with alerts when a forecast pushes a slope or a channel past its threshold
  • Additional pilot regions by partner and funder priority
  • Open data-access layer and reproducible outputs for researchers
  • Further hazard types as the FEM lineage extends

Infrastructure: scaled hosting and storage as coverage grows.

How to get involved

Engagement models

A non-exclusive, open-science initiative. HPC access and funded research partnerships help most — all routes below are open.

High Performance Computing (HPC) & cloud in-kind

HPC allocations or cloud credits covering the Phase 1 FEM workloads.

Research grant co-application

Named partner or host institution on hazard, climate-resilience, or open-science calls.

University & data partnership

HPC time, validation data, or supervision — in return, a live research testbed and open teaching resources.

Institutional sponsorship

Civil-protection agencies, NGOs, or foundations seeking public-benefit early warning in underserved regions.

Membership (£49.99 a year) pays for the hosting of what is live. It is not, and is not presented as, the financing mechanism for continuous forecasting, which is a compute cost of a different order.
