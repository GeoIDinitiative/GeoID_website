GeoHUB Guide

Using GeoHUB Earth

Navigating The Globe

Orbit

Left-click and drag to rotate the globe freely in any direction.

Zoom

Scroll or pinch, or hold the ‹ › ends of the zoom pill (top right). Tile basemaps sharpen automatically as you descend.

Pan

Right-click and drag (or two-finger drag on a trackpad) to pan the camera target.

Time

The corner pill cycles LIVE → ×720 → paused; the digital clock opens a scrubber. Set your display timezone in Settings — data and fetch dates stay UTC.

The Nav Bar — A Subject Taxonomy

The tab column files everything by what it IS, two tiers deep. Live is what is happening now: earthquake, volcano, wildfire, flood and storm feeds, switched on by subject. Explorer holds the globe itself — search, tour mode, locations, the Moon viewer and core cutaway. Basemaps dresses the sphere: the base-texture list (one at a time), the Overlays catalogue of reference shapes (graticule, borders, countries, submarine cables, elevation contours) and the terrain relief slider. Geology carries the world geological map, tectonics (plates, active faults, the World Stress Map) and the Smithsonian volcano catalogue. Earth System Observation nests Atmosphere (live weather maps and atmospheric datasets), Hydrology (sea level, rivers, coastlines) and the live Satellites tracker. Hazards leads with the myGeoID Factor-of-Safety pipeline beside burned-area and hazard-map tooling. Model Builder and Metadata close the column.

The Workspace

The corner box is your working set, always on screen while the nav bar is. Its header carries three icon doorways: + adds local data (shapefile, GeoJSON, KML, GPX, CSV/XYZ points, GeoTIFF, ASCII grid, STL/Gmsh/OBJ/PLY meshes — the dialog adapts to the file, maps CSV columns, and asks for an optional classification and note), the cloud requests a Google Earth Engine dataset over an extent, and the tray opens Export. Every layer row below shows an eye, opacity, draw order and a coloured type chip; a drawer per row holds symbology, rename, remove and To Model. Your own uploads and drawn shapes stay reclassifiable forever — prebuilt datasets wear a fixed chip.

Everything you draw or fetch lands here as an ordinary layer — reusable as a fetch extent by name, saved with the open project, and readable by the Model Builder and Research Hub.

Tools On The Rail

Distance / Draw / Profile

Distance measures great-circle arcs. Draw drags out boxes, circles, polygons and lines — the shape wears its name, area and W × H km, and Done files it as a study area. Profile draws an elevation cross-section (its A/B letters key the chart).

Points

Arm, click the globe to drop numbered points, Done files the set as a layer — sample sites without hand-typing a CSV.

Process / Analysis

Workbenches for pre-processing and for extraction & analysis — clipping, zonal statistics, extraction over the study area, CSV export.

Settings / Search

The gear holds Earth Engine credentials, the service endpoint and the clock timezone. Search (or /) finds every tool by name.

Fetching By Extent

Every fetcher asks the same question — which patch of ground? The extent picker offers the whole globe, the current view, any drawn shape by name, or any loaded layer’s bounding box. The ✎ draw-area button arms the Draw tool and claims the shape you draw; a fetch over a live drawn shape keeps it as a named “Fetch extent” layer for next time.

Keyboard Shortcuts

Toggle globe rotation on/off (except while typing in a field).
Esc
Close the active popup, cancel a drawing or point set, exit a GIS mode, or close any open modal.

Basemaps & GEBCO Elevation

The globe opens on the Sentinel-2 Cloudless mosaic (EOX, non-commercial licence — the source line under the catalogue always names what is on the sphere). OpenStreetMap, Esri imagery, NASA VIIRS daily and the shipped Blue Marble sit one tick away. Vertical exaggeration displaces the surface using the GEBCO 2025 global grid (~450 m resolution, Mariana Trench to Everest); the slider sets the exaggeration, and hillshade and slope layers derive from the same grid.

Live Data

The Live tab merges USGS seismicity (three windows on purpose — day, week, significant month), NASA EONET events, GDACS floods and NASA FIRMS fire detections from both MODIS and VIIRS, with US fire perimeters from NIFC. The Satellites section (under Earth System Observation) propagates ~1,700 spacecraft live from CelesTrak elements — dots at true altitude, orbit rings, clickable labels. Every feed is keyless, browser-direct and credited.

Suggested Workflow

Pick a basemap and relief. Draw your study area — it annotates itself with its size and lands in the Workspace. Fetch what the study needs: catalogue ticks for reference layers, the cloud doorway for Earth Engine datasets over your shape, + for your own files, the Points tool for sites. Classify inputs as they arrive so the pipeline can tell them apart. Style through each row’s Symbology, read values with Extract, then Export — or hand the shape To Model and continue in the Model Builder and Research Hub. Treat each layer as one line of evidence rather than a definitive answer.
