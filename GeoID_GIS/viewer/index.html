GeoHUB Guide

Using GeoHUB

Getting Around

Orbit

Left-click and drag to turn the globe. It spins with real time until you stop it.

Zoom

Scroll or pinch, or hold the − / + ends of the pill in the bottom-right corner, which names how far in you are. Imagery and elevation sharpen as you descend.

Time

The LIVE pill beside the world's name cycles live → ×720 → paused. Click the clock at the top to scrub to any moment. Fetch dates stay in UTC; Settings sets the display timezone.

Three pages

The GIS · Model · Research switch at the top of the nav bar changes the whole screen: the globe, the Meshing Studio, or the Research Hub. What you loaded stays loaded.

The Nav Bar

Tabs file everything by what it is, each opening into subtabs:

Live Events

What is happening now: earthquakes, volcanic activity, wildfires, ice, storms and floods (USGS, NASA EONET, GDACS), switched on by subject.

Explorer

Search, Locations, Tour Mode, Weather, Regions, the Moon viewer — and Core View, which cuts the planet open. Every layer on the globe is cut with it.

Map

Basemaps (Sentinel-2, VIIRS, OpenStreetMap, GEBCO), Elevation (a streamed 30 m DEM with slope and hillshade, plus vertical exaggeration and contours) and reference Overlays.

Geology

World geology, tectonics (plates, active faults, stress), soils and soil thickness, surface lithology, glaciers and ice sheets, volcanoes, and maps of rock properties (strength, stiffness, permeability).

Earth Observation

Atmosphere (live weather maps and Earth Engine datasets), Hydrology (coastlines, rivers, lakes, gauges, sea level) and a live satellite tracker.

Hazards

The myGeoID factor-of-safety mode, tropical cyclones, seismic and volcanic hazards, landslide forecasting, wildfires, floods and population exposure.

Model Builder

Six steps from a drawn study area to a meshable 3D or 2D model — see below.

Metadata

Where every layer came from: sources, licences and provenance, layer by layer.

The Workspace

The corner box is your working set. Its header holds four doorways: + adds your own data (shapefile, GeoJSON, KML, GPX, CSV/XYZ points, GeoTIFF, ASCII grid, meshes); the cloud browses Earth Engine's catalogue and draws a fetch extent; the tray exports; the gear opens Settings (theme and sounds, clock timezone, Earth Engine).

Each layer row has an eye, an opacity slider and drag-to-reorder. Its drawer holds Symbology, the attribute Table, Export (shapefile with a QGIS style, GeoJSON, KML, CSV…), To project and Remove. An ⓘ on a modelled layer opens the equations it was calculated with.

Everything you draw, fetch or compute lands here as an ordinary layer — usable as an extent by name, as an input to any tool, saved with the open project, and readable by the Model Builder and the Research Hub.

Cards, Legend and Events

Click any feature, event or map cell and a card opens beside it: a geological unit, a volcano, an earthquake (with its seismogram), a storm track or a risk cell. A card follows its dot as you move the globe, and closes with ✕, Esc, a click on empty ground — or on its own when the layer it describes is hidden or removed.

The Legend and Events buttons share the top-right corner, one drop-down at a time. Click an event in the list and the globe flies to it.

Drawing and Measuring

Draw

The Draw button on the rail raises the draw bar: circle, triangle, square, rectangle, pentagon, hexagon, or your own vertices. Press and drag on the globe; the shape shows its name, area and W × H km. Done (or Enter) files it as a study area.

Points, Distance, Profile

Also on the draw bar: drop sample points as a layer, click out a line whose every leg states its length, or draw a terrain profile. Export CSV appears on the bar while a measurement is up.

Geoprocessing · Analysis

Two workbenches on the rail: tools that make a map layer (clip, buffer, slope, watershed, viewshed, kriging, rasterize…) and tools that make tables and plots (zonal statistics, histograms, validation).

Search

The magnifier (or /) finds any of about fifty tools by name and opens it.

Records That Play

Tick a layer that has a history and a time bar opens under the globe: 13,513 tropical cyclone tracks since 1842 (storm by storm, month or season), 312,500 earthquakes back to the year 1008 (with magnitude bands to tick off), cyclone risk as the record grows, volcanic ashfall risk stepped VEI 1 to 8, glacier change and imagery over time. The bar has play, step, a speed pill and a scale of years; the world clock stands aside while it plays.

Hazard and Risk Maps

Cyclones

The chance of a storm passing within 200 km of anywhere, as a return period — any storm, or hurricane force only.

Earthquakes

Damaging shaking per year, by magnitude band, from the merged record.

Volcanoes

Hazard zones round every active volcano, and the chance of 1 mm of ash from eruptions of each size — from the modern record or the full Holocene.

Landslides

A forecast pipeline: pick an area and dates, fetch rainfall (GFS forecast or ERA5 archive), and it runs slope, geology, soil depth and a hydrogeological bucket to a factor of safety, hour by hour.

Model Builder and Meshing Studio

Six steps: choose the study area, give layers roles, build the terrain surface from the streamed DEM (uniform, or finer inside buffers you draw), set the domain — a 3D block with subsurface and atmosphere, a 2D surface, or a 2D cross-section along a line — add boundary conditions, points and mesh size fields, then build.

The package is written into the open project: STL files for each domain, gmsh scripts with named physical groups, a mesh size field and a run spec. Open in the Meshing Studio takes it to the Model page, where every face and point can be clicked, given a flag and meshed.

Knowing What You Are Looking At

Every catalogue row has an ⓘ: what the dataset is, who made it, its licence, and — for anything modelled — the equations. Cards say when a value is measured and when it is modelled, and a grid finer than its source says it is interpolated rather than pretending to a precision the data does not have.

Keyboard Shortcuts

Space
Pause or resume the globe's rotation (except while typing in a field).
Esc
Close the open card, cancel a drawing, or close a window.
Enter
File a finished drawing or point set; confirm a search or a field.
/
Open the tool search.

Suggested Workflow

Pick a basemap. Draw your study area — it names itself and lands in the Workspace. Load what the study needs from the tabs (geology, elevation, hazards, live feeds), or fetch more with the cloud doorway over that same area. Click around: every card says where its data came from. Run tools from the rail, export the results, or send the area to the Model Builder to build a mesh — and keep notes and analysis in the Research Hub.
