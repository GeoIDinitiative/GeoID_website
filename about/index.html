The GeoID Initiative

Two Platforms, One Scientific Backbone

GeoID is built around two distinct but complementary tracks. The Explorer platforms are live now — educational and exploratory tools that make world-class geoscience accessible to anyone without institutional access or specialist training. myGeoID is the operational horizon: a proposed real-time geohazard risk application that turns the same scientific foundation into actionable public safety intelligence.

Understanding which track you are engaging with matters. Explorer is for learning; myGeoID is for acting. The first is open today; the second is in development and requires funding and collaboration to realise. We aim to give the fish, not just the fishing rod — but first we need your support and external funding to access the resources required to build a first of its kind, people-first, geohazard intelligence platform.

What We're Building Toward

Making Hazard Intelligence Accessible

World-class geoscience already exists — the problem is that it is stuck within an insular community, undermined by politics and rarely fed directly to the general public. GeoID is working to close that gap: taking the same physics-based models and datasets used by research institutions and making them accessible to anyone, anywhere. The Explorer viewers are live today; the hazard forecasting platform is in development and seeking collaborators.

Global Reach, Local Clarity

From the Andes to the Himalayas, from coastal Ireland to the Philippine archipelago — myGeoID is designed to deliver a continuously updated Factor of Safety for landslide and flood risk at any location on Earth. One signal, instantly interpretable, no training required.

Forecast-Driven by Design

By ingesting NOAA GFS global weather forecast data, myGeoID is designed to anticipate rising instability before it becomes visible — giving communities, insurers, and first responders the lead time to act. The methodology is defined; realising it at scale requires investment and partnerships.

Evidence You Can Act On

The goal is a transparent, independently published risk index that lets individuals challenge insurers, hold authorities accountable, and make informed decisions — backed by the same evidence base experts use. Open methodology is a core principle from day one.

Our Approach

The GeoID Ecosystem

Two live educational platforms and one in-development operational platform — all built on the same scientific foundation of Earth observation data, physics-based modelling, and machine learning. Planet Explorer and Earth Explorer are open for membership today. myGeoID represents where that foundation is heading: actionable, real-time geohazard intelligence for the public.

Live Now

Planet Explorer

An interactive Solar System GIS environment for learning and exploration. Users can explore planetary surfaces, visualise geodynamic datasets, and engage with the natural world at scales from local terrain to Solar System bodies — no prior GIS expertise required.

The Moon viewer is freely available to all; the full suite of planetary environments — Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto — unlocks with a GeoID membership. Planet Explorer is the large-scale face of GeoID Explorer.

In Development Open for Collaboration

myGeoID App

A living repository of real-time data and mapping streams that ingests NOAA GFS global forecast data to produce a continuously updated Factor of Safety index for landslide and flood risk — delivered as a clean, mobile-first risk tracker inspired by financial market dashboards.

myGeoID is actively seeking academic and industrial collaboration and funding. If you are a researcher, insurer, NGO, or civil protection authority, we want to hear from you.

Live Now

Earth Explorer

Immersive 3D viewers for Earth's most geologically significant regions — active volcanoes, transform fault systems, canyon landscapes, and ice sheets. Built from published scientific datasets and running entirely in your browser.

Navigate subsurface fault architecture, cross-section the terrain, explore real seismicity, and interrogate geological units at the scale of a research-grade GIS — no software to install, no prior expertise required.
