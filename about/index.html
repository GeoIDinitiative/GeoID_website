How it works

The Record, the Ground, and the Forecast

The catalogues research institutions work from are public but scattered. GeoHUB merges them onto one globe, adds the terrain and geology underneath, and runs its hazard models on that ground with the next sixteen days of forecast rainfall.

The record, merged

USGS ComCat, ISC-GEM and GEM GHEC merged into one earthquake record of 312,500 events; every IBTrACS cyclone track; Macrostrat geology; Smithsonian volcanoes — each layer keeping its licence and its DOI.

Forecast-driven by design

Sixteen days of hourly NOAA GFS rainfall drive a water balance over the real drainage and an infinite-slope Factor of Safety per cell — a stack of maps the clock plays through, not one susceptibility picture.

Methods you can check

Every model states its assumptions and sources beside the map, and the methods are written up as technical notes in the Journal, with a citation and a DOI where one exists.

Our Approach

The GeoID Ecosystem

GeoHUB is the platform, and everything GeoID builds is part of it: one window and one project, with three facets working on the same ground. A study area drawn on the globe is the model in the studio and the figure in the write-up, with no export in between. The globe, the studio and the hub are free; membership adds the six hazard models, rock properties, the Model Builder and Earth Engine inside it.

GeoHUB

01 · GIS

Live · free Hazard models · membership

Earth & Planet Explorer

A GIS on a 3D globe, with the same tools on Earth and nine more worlds. Many features on one sphere: the earthquake and cyclone record played through time, world geology, live events and satellites, weather radar, Earth Engine layers and the hazard maps, drawn over study areas you define, clicked into, sampled and exported.

Close-up models of Etna and Everest open from the globe.

02 · Model

Live · free

Meshing Studio

A study area becomes a finite-element model in the same tab: the terrain sampled into subsurface and atmosphere volumes, or a model built from primitives; faces flagged, meshed with gmsh, materials and boundary conditions set.

Solved with GALES on your machine or a server, and read back as slices, stresses, InSAR fringes and fits to observations.

03 · Research

Live · free

Research Hub

A project that ingests, analyses and publishes: time series from the globe and the solver, signal processing, spectral analysis and statistics, plots, figures, notes and linked Google Docs and Sheets.

A project is a real folder, in the same layout as the desktop application.

Work with GeoID

Use It, Check It, Build On It

A membership keeps the globe free for everyone else. Beyond that, the roadmap — continuous hosted forecasting and region-by-region validation — is built with partners.

Ways to Get Involved

01

Researchers & Universities

Run the hazard models over your region, test them against your inventories, and co-author the validation notes in the Journal.

02

Public Authorities & NGOs

Pilot the landslide and flood models over an area you are responsible for, with the method open beside every map.

03

Developers & Data Scientists

The site is static files and stdlib Python, with no build step; the Research Hub runs GALES through a local sidecar. Bring a catalogue, a solver or a pipeline.

04

Partners & Sponsors

Hosted compute and HPC time are what moves the models from on-demand to continuous. The roadmap sets out the engagement routes.
