Using The Earth Explorer

Navigating The Globe

Left-click and drag to rotate the globe freely in any direction.

Control Panel Layout

The left panel runs from navigation to interpretation. Navigate holds search and reset. Tour Mode steps through curated stop sequences. Locations controls surface labels and launch-site markers by category. Basemap & Relief sets the globe texture and terrain displacement. Geology overlays the GEBCO-derived bathymetry context. The panels below add sea level, region-mask, and core cutaway views. The cursor bar shows live latitude, longitude, and elevation as you explore.

GEBCO Elevation Data

The elevation data used for terrain relief is derived from the GEBCO 2025 global bathymetry and topography grid. GEBCO (General Bathymetric Chart of the Oceans) provides one of the most comprehensive global elevation datasets, covering both the ocean floor and land surface at a resolution of 15 arc-seconds (~450 m at the equator).

The terrain scale slider controls how much the elevation data displaces the globe surface. Increase it to exaggerate mountains and ocean trenches, or reduce it for a flatter appearance. The hillshade and slope basemap layers are also derived from the same GEBCO 2025 dataset.

Ocean depth is visualised by pushing the ocean floor down relative to the land surface. The slider range shows the full topographic range from Mariana Trench (~10,930 m below sea level) to Everest (~8,849 m above).

Tour Mode

Enable Tour Mode from the panel and choose a theme — landforms, oceans, volcanoes, or launch sites. The viewer flies automatically to each stop in sequence. Use the Prev and Next buttons or the target dropdown to navigate. Your basemap, overlays, and sea-level setting are preserved across stops and carry forward when you exit tour mode. Switching theme mid-tour keeps your current configuration.

Moon Viewer

Select the Moon from the Moon Viewer section to fly out to it in close orbit. The panel focuses to show lunar feature labels — crater names, mare plains, and impact basins — and the Earth surface labels scale proportionally in the background. Click any Moon feature label for its description and orbital data. Exit Moon viewer with the close button or by clicking an Earth surface feature in search.

Datasets Available

Global basemaps

NASA Earth surface texture, GEBCO-derived hillshade, and GEBCO-derived slope basemap.

Elevation and bathymetry

GEBCO 2025 global grid covering ocean floor and land surface at ~450 m resolution, from the Mariana Trench (−10,930 m) to Everest (+8,849 m).

Geology and relief context

GEBCO-derived relief context layer showing bathymetric and topographic structure, with optional contact and structure overlays.

Named features

Key landforms, ocean basins, mountain belts, volcanic systems, storm reference zones, and space launch sites.

Sea level tool

Drag the sea-level slider to explore inundation and exposure scenarios. Useful for visualising ice-age low-stands or future sea-level rise contexts.

Interior cutaway

Enable Core View to see Earth's layered interior — crust, upper and lower mantle, and iron-nickel core — in a schematic cross-section.

Measurements & Tools

Open the Tools panel from the right-hand tab. Point distance measures the great-circle arc between two surface clicks. Area measurement computes the enclosed polygon area across the surface. The elevation profile tool draws a cross-section along a dragged path. All measurement results can be exported as CSV or PNG.

Suggested Workflow

Start with the NASA Earth surface texture for visual context. Add terrain relief to read ocean depth and mountain elevation. Enable the GEBCO context layer to identify bathymetric structure. Use the sea-level slider to visualise glacial low-stands or sea-level rise inundation scenarios. Switch on the Core View to explore Earth's layered interior. For each layer, treat it as one line of evidence rather than a definitive answer.
