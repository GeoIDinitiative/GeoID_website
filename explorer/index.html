What is the Planet Explorer?

Nine planetary viewers, Mercury to Pluto — each a dedicated scientific environment built from mission data: MOLA relief and CTX imagery for Mars, Magellan radar for Venus, MESSENGER maps for Mercury, LROC mosaics for the Moon. All in your browser, no installation, no training. Alongside Earth Explorer and myGeoID, it is GeoID's proof that the distance between a curious mind and a planet is seconds.

Explore our Solar System

🚀

A different kind of planetary interface

Most platforms show you a planet. Planet Explorer lets you read one: real scientific datasets — topography, geological units, atmospheric imagery, mineral layers — toggled, stacked, and compared in one session. No institutional access, no months of training.

Built for researchers and first-timers equally

A researcher cross-referencing Martian valley networks uses the same viewer as a student asking why Olympus Mons is so vast — by design. Guided stops give footholds; layer controls let you build your own picture of a world.

CTX mosaic streaming — superresolution without the hardware

The Mars viewer streams the full Context Camera mosaic tile by tile — sub-metre imagery of the whole surface, no local processing. Pan Valles Marineris down to individual landslide scarps; zoom into Jezero Crater as the rover landing team did.

Planetary geology datasets and the raster calculator

Peer-reviewed geological unit datasets — the same data researchers publish from — sit as toggleable layers over every rocky world. The raster calculator raises or lowers a simulated ocean in real time: flood Mars's northern lowlands, trace the ancient coastlines scientists still argue over.

The Lunar & Moon Viewer

🌖

Earth's Moon — the flagship viewer

The LROC 8 K colour mosaic over the LOLA elevation model, with two thousand-plus IAU-catalogued features readable off the surface — craters, maria, montes, and the Apollo, Luna, Chang'e and Chandrayaan landing sites. Every label opens a scientific description.

USGS Unified Geologic Map — every unit mapped, every contact selectable

Toggle the Unified Geologic Map of the Moon (Skinner et al., 2020): twelve thousand-plus units colour-coded by stratigraphic era, each clickable for unit code and rock type. A static globe becomes an interactive geological map.

An interior cutaway built from Apollo seismology

The Core View slices the Moon from crust to inner core using the Apollo seismic reanalysis (Weber et al. 2011; Garcia et al. 2023) — all 1,737 km to scale, every layer clickable.

Earth–Moon system context

The Moon sits in its real orbital system with Earth in the background — tidal locking and libration made legible.

Beyond Earth — the moons of the other planets

Every planet has a moon-system viewer of its own — Phobos and Deimos to the Galilean moons, Saturn's family, the Uranian satellites, Triton, and the Pluto–Charon binary — each with surface imagery and a selectable feature catalogue.
