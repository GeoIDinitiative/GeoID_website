What is the Earth Explorer?

Earth Explorer is GeoID's regional Earth science platform — a growing suite of high-resolution 3D environments built around specific geologically significant sites across the planet. Where Planet Explorer surveys entire worlds at global scale, Earth Explorer goes deeper into defined geographic domains: a volcano, a fault zone, a caldera system — each rendered at the resolution and scientific detail the site's own geological complexity demands.

The platform is built on the same open-science foundation as all GeoID tools: no institutional gatekeeping, no specialist prerequisites, and no gap between the data researchers use and what the public can access. The Mount Etna viewer is live now and accessible via the annual membership. Future environments currently under consideration include the Campi Flegrei caldera system, the Reykjanes Peninsula volcanic zone in Iceland, the Aegean arc (Santorini–Milos), and Himalayan seismic corridors — each selected for their scientific significance and the availability of high-quality open datasets to support them.

A different kind of geoscience interface

Most Earth science tools give you one of two things: professional GIS software that demands institutional access, specialist training, and months of onboarding before it yields any insight — or educational apps that reduce active geology to passive imagery, beautiful but entirely disconnected from the underlying science. Earth Explorer was built to sit in neither category. Each viewer is a fully layered investigation environment where peer-reviewed datasets — seismic tomography, geological unit maps, fault system architectures, real earthquake catalogues, satellite terrain — can be toggled, stacked, and explored in a single coherent session. The interface is not a simplified substitute for scientific software. It is a rethinking of how that software should have worked from the beginning.

Real science, directly interactive

Every layer in Earth Explorer is drawn from published, peer-reviewed sources. Subsurface magmatic structures are derived from seismic tomography and geodetic inversion models published in the primary literature. Fault architectures are taken from national geological survey databases. Earthquake hypocentres come from the INGV catalogue — the same dataset seismologists analyse. Basemap imagery is sourced from ESRI World Imagery at approximately 7 m resolution, pre-rendered into a local tile cache. These are not illustrative approximations. They are the actual datasets researchers publish from, rendered in real time, in your browser, with no software installation required.

Full 3D terrain navigation in the browser

The viewer renders a full three-dimensional terrain model of the region — summit to coastline — using WebGL, with 7 m-resolution ESRI World Imagery satellite tiles pre-rendered into a local cache. Orbit, zoom, and pan with the mouse. Raise vertical exaggeration to read subtle topographic gradients. Switch between satellite, hillshade, and slope-angle basemaps in a single click. Open the subsurface cross-section to expose the magmatic plumbing system below the surface. Everything responds in real time, making exploration feel continuous rather than constrained — because the data no longer has to be downloaded before it can be understood.

A platform that scales with your curiosity

A researcher cross-referencing fault kinematics with seismicity depth distributions uses exactly the same viewer as a student trying to understand what a stratovolcano is and why it erupts. That is not a coincidence — it is the central design principle. Labelled geological features open scientific descriptions written to be equally rewarding to a first-time visitor and to a volcanologist. Layer controls and filtering tools allow users to build their own picture of the landscape incrementally, the way a field geoscientist approaches a new site: with evidence, iteration, and curiosity. The more you put in, the more Earth Explorer gives back.

Explore Europe's Most Active Volcano, Mount Etna, in 3D

LIVE

About

Europe's largest and most active stratovolcano — rendered as a fully interactive 3D geological model built from real published datasets. Navigate from summit crater to the Mediterranean coastline across a 100 km domain, in real time, entirely in your browser.

Subsurface Structure

Three resolved magma chambers — shallow, intermediate, and deep — connected by conduits and sill complexes derived from published seismic tomography and geodetic models. Toggle each body independently and section through the edifice with the cross-section tool.

Fault Architecture & Seismicity

All active fault systems mapped at surface and subsurface extent. Real INGV earthquake catalogue overlaid as depth-coloured spheres, filterable by magnitude and time period.

High-Resolution Basemap

7 m-resolution ESRI World Imagery satellite tiles, pre-rendered into a local tile cache across the full domain, with switchable hillshade and slope-angle basemaps — all scaling dynamically with vertical exaggeration.
