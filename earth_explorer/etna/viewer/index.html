Using The Etna 3D Viewer

About This Model

Surface topography from a 100 × 100 km DEM. Seismic stations from the INGV monitoring network (Osservatorio Etneo). Satellite imagery from ESRI World Imagery.

The subsurface magmatic plumbing geometry is speculative. It is a visualisation of chamber geometry estimates derived from published seismic and geodetic studies and does not represent a formally validated model. Chamber positions, dimensions, and conduit networks are approximations consistent with the literature cited below, not outputs of a numerical inversion.

The Plumbing System

The model depicts a multi-reservoir magmatic system feeding the summit craters: a shallow spherical body (~1.5 km asl), a prolate intermediate reservoir (~2 km depth), a melt-bearing lens at ~5 km, an oblate mid-crustal storage zone at ~10 km, and a deep magma reservoir at ~18 km depth coupled to the subducting Sicilian Chain slab. A sill complex between 0–8 km represents lateral storage inferred from seismicity patterns. Interconnecting conduits (~50 m diameter) trace inferred ascent pathways.

This geometry is a schematic visualisation based on chamber depth and dimension estimates from the literature. It is not the output of a formal geophysical inversion.

Key sources:
Firetto Carlino et al. (2025). Earthquake frequency-magnitude distribution at Mount Etna sheds light on magma ascent in the volcano's plumbing system. Science Advances, 11(41), eadx9873. doi:10.1126/sciadv.adx9873
Cannata et al. (2018). Space–time evolution of magma storage and transfer at Mt. Etna Volcano (Italy): The 2015–2016 reawakening of Voragine Crater. Geochemistry, Geophysics, Geosystems, 19(2), 471–495. doi:10.1002/2017GC007296

Cross-Section

Enable Cross-Section from the control panel to slice through all model components simultaneously with a vertical cutting plane. Rotate the slice angle to reveal E–W, N–S, or oblique cross-sections through the volcano and its subsurface plumbing. This is equivalent to the Core View function in the planet explorers — the same Three.js clipping plane technique is used.

Seismic Stations

Cyan spheres mark INGV monitoring stations from the Osservatorio Etneo network. Station positions are given in the GMSH domain coordinate system and converted to Three.js coordinates. Toggle station visibility and labels from the Labels section.

Coordinate System

All distances in the viewer are in kilometres. The origin (0, 0, 0) is at the centre of the 100 × 100 km GMSH domain at sea level — approximately above the summit crater area. Positive Y is above sea level; negative Y is depth below sea level. The surface mesh extends from the domain boundary to the summit at approximately Y = +3.3 km.
